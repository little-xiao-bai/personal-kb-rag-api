Haddington, East Lothian

The name Haddington is Anglo-Saxon, dating from the sixth or seventh century AD when the area was incorporated into the kingdom of Bernicia.

The town, like the rest of the Lothian region, was ceded by King Edgar of England and became part of Scotland in the tenth century.

Haddington received burghal status, one of the earliest to do so, during the reign of David I (1124–1153), giving it trading rights which encouraged its growth into a market town.
